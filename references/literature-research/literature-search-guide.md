# Literature Search Guide (PA Case Study Research)

Goal: build a targeted “theory dialogue” around your RQ/mechanism chain (not a citation pile).

---

## 1) Start from a 3-sentence problem statement

Write (short and specific):
1) Phenomenon/pattern to explain: **[...]**
2) Puzzle/gap: existing accounts miss **[mechanism link / boundary condition / concept distinction]**
3) Theory conversation: you will contribute by specifying **[mechanism/propositions/boundaries]**

---

## 2) Build keyword pools by mechanism chain

Use condition–actor–mechanism–outcome pools:
- conditions: performance regime, fiscal stress, crisis, reform, central mandates, fragmentation, etc.
- actors: street-level bureaucrats, local governments, agencies, networks, citizens, etc.
- mechanisms: discretion, collaboration, accountability, learning, coproduction, compliance, etc.
- outcomes: implementation gap, service delivery, innovation, equity, trust, etc.

Maintain:
- English keyword set (for databases),
- localized keywords (for domestic journals, dissertations, policy reports).

---

## 3) Channel priority (recommended)

1) Google Scholar (discovery + cited-by / references chaining)
2) Web of Science / Scopus (systematic search + filters)
3) Target journals’ internal search (PAR/JPART/Governance/PAQ/PMR, etc.)
4) High-quality review articles and book chapters (to anchor concepts and debates)

---

## 4) Screening: relevance -> authority -> usability

- relevance: does it speak to your mechanism links/boundaries?
- authority: is it a field anchor (frequently cited; journal quality; canonical framing)?
- usability: can it support your definitions and the specific claims you make?

---

## 5) Write a dialogue map (1-page table)

Recommended table columns:
- citation (author-year)
- what it explains
- core mechanism claim
- boundary conditions
- how you use it (borrow / refine / challenge / extend)
